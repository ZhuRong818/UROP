# EventX baseline follow-up

Research review: 12 October 2026. This extends the [10 October comparison](MARKET_NEWS_EXTERNAL_BASELINE_COMPARISON_20261010.md). It is a literature review, not a model run. Primary papers and publisher or author abstracts were checked; the two new SSRN findings below are abstract-level evidence only.

## Exact task and comparability

EventX forecasts the probability that a binary prediction-market contract's **absolute log-odds change over the next 30 minutes** crosses its trailing-volatility threshold, at five-minute decision times. The original threshold is `sigma > 0 and abs(logodds(t+30m)-logodds(t)) >= 4*sigma`, where `sigma` comes from trailing one-minute changes. The score is binary-event log loss, Brier and average precision. See the [V5 protocol](eventx/release/v5/PROTOCOL_V1_1.md).

The reviewed literature contains **no verified paper with the same target, horizon, data clock, evaluation population and metrics**. This is a bounded search finding, not proof that no such study exists. Current YES price is the natural reference for **eventual resolution**, but it is not a probability of a large price movement. Its Brier score cannot be compared to ours.

## Closest external baseline families

| Source | Published task and evidence | Difference from EventX | Usable baseline idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Archak & Ipeirotis, *Modeling Volatility in Prediction Markets* (2009)](https://ipeirotis.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/01/ec2009.pdf) | InTrade contract volatility; structural price/time-to-expiry volatility, GARCH(1,1) and structural-normalized GARCH. The latter led 26/51 held-half contracts; structural alone beat plain GARCH in 17/25 remaining contracts. | Continuous return likelihood on 2008 election contracts, not 30-minute threshold-event log loss. Model diffusion misses sudden information jumps and assumes a known expiration clock. | Fit an EWMA/GARCH and structural volatility family on our training folds; convert its forecast distribution into threshold-crossing probability and calibrate on training folds. |
| [Hou, Li & Zheng, *The Cross-Section of Price Changes in Prediction Markets* (2026 working paper)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7365378) | Author abstract reports 96,860 Kalshi contracts, subsequent appreciation by YES-side market value and reversal of one-to-six-day log-odds moves, strongest next day. | Signed cross-sectional returns over days; no verified 30-minute event probabilities, loss scores or news ablation. | Add a transparent market-only reversal/momentum specification; test whether any effect persists at 30 minutes. Abstract-level only. |
| [Li & Luan, *What Moves Prediction Markets? Evidence from Corporate Earnings* (2026 working paper)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7324239) | Author abstract reports 599 Polymarket earnings events. High-frequency stock returns predict prediction-market changes over one hour to one day; analyst revisions and firm news are examined, and news arrival raises trading activity. | Narrow corporate domain, signed price changes and event-study relations, not pooled absolute-movement classification. Externally observed stock prices are structured signals unlike our delayed article feed. | In a new corporate pilot, compare market history with timestamped stock-return and analyst-surprise features, then add news content. Abstract-level only. |
| [Angelini & De Angelis, *When Do Markets Fully Process Public Information?* (2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07811) | One-minute Kalshi NBA midpoints and observable game states; an out-of-sample public win-probability benchmark measures incomplete immediate updating and predictive later drift, especially at low liquidity. | Several-minute signed adjustment to live structured game state, with midpoints; EventX uses a 30-minute unsigned target and execution prices. Their regression coefficients are not loss reductions. | For a separate delayed-repricing task, build a public-state surprise/gap model with liquidity controls. |
| [Halawi et al., *Approaching Human-Level Forecasting with Language Models* (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18563v1) and [Prophet Arena (2025 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17638v1) | Retrieval, reasoning and crowd/market comparison for **final outcomes**. Prophet Arena reports market Brier 0.187 and GPT-5 High 0.184 on its own event-weighted evaluation. | Final-resolution probabilities, different populations and weighting. Neither supplies a same-task 30-minute news lift over a nonlinear market model. | Use their retrieval/market/both ablation structure, not their scores as a performance bar. |

An earlier [publisher study by Sung et al. (2019)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377221718305575) also motivates price-trend correction, but its target is final event probability and a verified numerical improvement was unavailable in our review. [Khan & Lieli's 2010 preliminary study](https://economics.ucr.edu/repec/ucr/wpaper/201426.pdf) examines daily prediction-market prices versus poll surprises and media volume; it is a useful information-flow precedent, not a forecasting benchmark, and the draft itself asks not to be cited without permission.

## What our present results establish

On 364,903 opened development rows, the matched nonlinear market tree reduced log loss from **0.451070 to 0.407391** versus L2 logistic regression (**9.683467%**). This is an exploratory local algorithm comparison after search. On 5,388 matched news-exposed fold-5 rows, the stronger market-only histogram booster scored **0.243727** versus **0.244576** for the selected timing-news arm; the news arm was worse by **0.000849 nats per row**. This does not rule out a useful gain from validated contract-specific news. Exact source values and hashes are in [evidence.json](eventx/release/literature_baselines_20261010/evidence.json).

## Benchmark recommendation

For a same-task development benchmark, compare on identical decision keys and forward folds: (1) training-only event rate; (2) transparent price, time, momentum/reversal, activity and freshness logistic model; (3) EWMA/GARCH and Archak-style structural volatility converted to threshold-event probabilities; (4) selected nonlinear market-only booster; (5) market plus feed exposure and timing; (6) that model plus reviewed contract-specific signed surprise. Estimate all calibration and residual tails inside each training window. Report both entire eligible population and news-exposed subset, with log loss, Brier, AP and calibration. A future untouched evaluation requires a separately frozen design and adequate independent event episodes. These recommendations do not change frozen V4 or authorize a new holdout consumption.
